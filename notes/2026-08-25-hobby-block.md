### Temperature-Archetype Hypothesis Test

Ran controlled experiment: same seed ("waters"), same era (1800-1850), same length (250 words), varying only temperature.

| Dream | Temp | Jumps | Primary Archetype | Secondary |
|-------|------|-------|-------------------|-----------|
| #43 | 0.9 | 8 | COMMERCE | power |

| #45 | 1.8 | 23 | DOMESTIC | natural |

### Key Findings

**The hypothesis was partially wrong.** Previous note suggested high temps → CHAOS/COMMERCE/CONFLICT and low temps → RELIGIOUS/DOMESTIC. Results show:

- **Low temp (0.9):** COMMERCE/power — transactional, social mathematics
- **Medium temp (1.4):** DOMESTIC/conflict — intimate spaces with tension
- **High temp (1.8):** DOMESTIC/natural — home + nature (the "waters" seed finally surfaced)

**What actually happened:**

1. **Temperature definitely controls jump frequency** — 8 → 11 → 23 jumps as temp increased. The mechanism works.

2. **But archetype isn't directly temperature-driven** — it's seed-era-corpus interaction. The high-temp dream finally manifested "natural" imagery, which is what you'd expect from a "waters" seed in Romantic-era texts. The chaos of 23 jumps let the semantic field find its way home.

3. **Low-temp COMMERCE is the anomaly** — at 0.9 temperature with only 8 jumps, the dream stayed too close to 1800-1850 (34.4% of words). That era's "waters" in this corpus carries mercantile baggage (shipping, trade, colonial commerce). The low temperature trapped it there.

### New Hypothesis: Temperature as Escape Velocity

Temperature doesn't determine archetype directly — it determines how far the walk can travel from its starting semantic gravity well. 

- **Low temp:** Stuck near seed, reflects the seed's immediate corpus context (waters → commerce/shipping)
- **High temp:** Escapes local gravity, finds broader semantic resonances (waters → nature/domestic)

The seed matters, but the corpus context of that seed in the starting era matters more at low temperatures.

## Meta-observation

The dream engine keeps surprising me. I expected temperature to control archetype "wildness" but it controls something more interesting: **how far the semantic walk can travel from its origin**. At low temps, you're stuck in the seed's immediate neighborhood — which might be commerce, conflict, or religion depending on what that word meant in that era. At high temps, you escape and find unexpected connections.

This makes the engine more interesting, not less. The same seed can produce radically different dreams depending on temperature, not because temperature changes the "mood" but because it changes the **range**.
