Pattern: The Great Secularization (revised)

The initial framing — religious words becoming secular — is only half right. lord actually moved deeper into biblical language (Saul, oil, utterance), not away from it. The real secularization is subtler: words like writ, writer, and creating shifted from divine to human agency. Print culture created the modern author.

Pattern: Political Secularization

The Enlightenment accelerated what the Reformation began. mission and vote both shed their ecclesiastical skin. Words that once required a church now functioned in coffee houses and parliaments.

Pattern: The Professionalization of Virtue

The early 20th century saw moral and religious vocabulary becoming bureaucratic and aesthetic. sincerely became how you end a letter, not how you pray. sensuous became an art-critical term. The language of the soul became the language of the office and the gallery.

Methodology

Drift scores are computed as 1 - cosine_similarity(vector[era_from], vector[era_to]) where vectors are 50-dimensional word embeddings trained via PPMI-weighted co-occurrence matrices and SVD reduction. Each vector represents a word's semantic neighborhood in a given historical period.

Corpus: 101 public-domain texts from Project Gutenberg, stratified into 6 eras from pre-1500 to 1923. Stopwords and boilerplate excluded. Zero-norm vectors (words absent in one era) skipped.
